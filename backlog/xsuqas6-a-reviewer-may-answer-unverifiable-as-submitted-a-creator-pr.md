# A reviewer may answer "unverifiable as submitted" — a creator-proof gap, not a verdict

A reviewer may answer **"unverifiable as submitted"** — distinct from accept/changes — when a claim about the creator's own code carries nothing a reviewer can cheaply check. It routes as a new `DISPOSITIONS` member in we:scripts/lib/jury-core.mjs, gated by a precondition question asked before the existing three-question blocker test (#2950), reusing [#3312](/backlog/3312-findings-are-admitted-by-evidence-kind/)'s `evidenceKind` ladder for what counts as proof. It earns a round like a blocker but is counted separately, so [#3318](/backlog/3318-review-efficacy-watch/)'s metrics tell "reviewer found a bug" apart from "creator skipped proof." The refusal is the durable signal, routed through two already-shipped pipelines — no new harvest mechanism.

Grounded in a prior-art survey published at [/research/creator-owed-proof-not-reviewer-rederivation/](/research/creator-owed-proof-not-reviewer-rederivation/) (session report [we:reports/2026-08-27-creator-owed-proof-burden-shift.md](reports/2026-08-27-creator-owed-proof-burden-shift.md)) plus this repo's own jury-core machinery, read directly rather than assumed:

- `DISPOSITIONS` (`blocker`/`carve-out`/`nit`, [we:scripts/lib/jury-core.mjs:284-299](scripts/lib/jury-core.mjs)) and `deriveFindingDisposition` (`:318-322`) route **three DIRECTION facts about a finding the reviewer has already determined is real** — did this change introduce it, is it worse than base, can it be fixed in parallel. `buildSubjectMandate`'s DISPOSITION block (`:1984-1999`) is where those three questions are put to every juror.
- `EVIDENCE_KINDS`/`EVIDENCE_STRENGTH` (`:653-680`), `classifyFindingEvidence` (`:783-803`) and `admitFindingsByEvidence` (`:839-870`) grade what a pure function can check about a **reviewer's own citation** ([#3312](/backlog/3312/)) — shipped, floor OFF by default. The ladder's top rung deliberately excludes a red-before/green-after test run, and says why: *"`test:unit` is ~693s against a 20-minute juror kill"* (`:649-651`) — a **reviewer-budget** constraint. That constraint does not bind a **creator**, who is not reviewing under a kill timer.
- [#3341](/backlog/3341-a-card-s-mechanism-claim-must-cite-the-function-or-line-that/) requires a citation for a mechanism claim ("X does Y by mechanism Z"); [#3362](/backlog/3362-state-a-check-s-predicate-and-candidate-set-never-assert-com/) requires a stated scan for a completeness claim ("every X does Y"). Both are narrow, already-shipped slices of the **same** "state your evidence, don't assert" discipline — gated on citation, not on whether the citation actually *proves* the claim. This item generalizes the discipline to code-behavior claims themselves, and turns it back reflexively on the **reviewer's own refusal** (Fork 1).
- [#3354](/backlog/3354-require-adversarial-fixtures-for-machinery-that-consumes-mod/) (ratified 2026-08-27) already amended [`#agent-convergence-independent-validation`](docs/agent/platform-decisions.md#agent-convergence-independent-validation) clause 2 with a model-consumer fixture requirement — the operator's own framing of this item as "the mirror of #3312: that grades the reviewer's evidence, this grades the creator's" is literal, not loose.

## Recommended path at a glance

| Fork | Recommended default | Main alternative | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fork 1 — where the line sits | Conjunctive test: load-bearing (a typed `impactIfUnfixed` bar) **and** cost-asymmetric **and** the reviewer states its own attempted verification | Cost-asymmetry alone (the operator's original candidate, unsharpened) | high |
| Fork 2 — grade a verified test above a citation? | Yes — extend `EVIDENCE_KINDS` with one new top rung (a caller-observed red-before/green-after result, fed as ground truth the way `sources`/`scope` already are); the enum-vs-field shape is a resolved default, not part of the fork | No — cap creator-proof evidence at the existing four rungs | med-high |

Two forced invariants sit outside the table (below the forks) — nothing to weigh, only to ratify.

## Fork 1 — What test gates a reviewer's use of "unverifiable as submitted"?

**Why this is a fork, not a forced invariant:** at least three coherent formulations exist, none obviously broken, and they trade real merit against each other — permissiveness vs. precision vs. long-run maintenance burden. This is exactly the axis the operator flagged as the hard part: *"define the line so this doesn't become 'the reviewer refuses everything.'"*

**The stakes.** Get this wrong in the permissive direction and every claim becomes refusable — a reviewer that would rather not look wins for free, and the whole mechanism degenerates into an excuse. Get it wrong in the narrow direction (an enumerated allowlist of claim *categories*) and it needs constant curation as new claim shapes appear, and it duplicates what #3341/#3362 already do narrowly.

- **(a) Cost-asymmetry alone** — "a claim is creator-owed proof when verifying it from scratch costs materially more than the creator including the proof would have cost" (the operator's own candidate, unsharpened). *Rejected as the sole test*: any claim can be framed as "would cost me time to check" — a lazy reviewer's "I'd have to look at three files" clears this bar as easily as a genuine load-bearing gap, which is precisely the "refuses everything" failure named as the constraint. Cost-asymmetry is real and load-bearing (kept below), but alone it has no floor.
- **(b) Conjunctive test — load-bearing AND cost-asymmetric AND stated-attempt.** **RECOMMENDED.** A finding may only carry `disposition: 'unverifiable'` when **all three** hold:
  1. **Load-bearing.** The reviewer declares `impactIfUnfixed` at or above a stated bar (a new named dial, `UNVERIFIABLE_IMPACT_BAR`, defaulting to `broken` — the same *value* `PREVENTION_IMPACT_BAR` uses today, but a **distinct constant** so the two gates can diverge later without coupling; `we:scripts/lib/jury-core.mjs:197-215` already carries the enum and glosses this reuses). A cosmetic or degraded claim stays plain assertion — advisory, never a refusal.
  2. **Cost-asymmetric.** Re-deriving the claim from scratch would cost the reviewer materially more than the creator including the proof would have cost. Kept as a judgment call, not a numeric threshold — the same shape as the existing three direction-tests (`introduced`/`worseThanBase`/`parallelizable`), which are booleans a reviewer answers honestly and the *routing* (code, not the model) decides on.
  3. **Attempted and stated.** The reviewer must name what it actually tried — what it checked, searched, or ran — and why that wasn't sufficient. Structurally: a new required field, `verificationAttempt` (non-empty string), shape-checked the way `reproCommand` already is by `isReproCommand` (`:760-767`) — *"it does NOT verify the attempt was reasonable, only that something was stated"* is the honest limit of a pure function here, exactly `isReproCommand`'s own stated limit for its class. An `unverifiable` declaration with no `verificationAttempt` is **incomplete**, which `deriveFindingDisposition`'s existing "answered" discipline (`:319-320`) already treats as **undecided → fails closed → blocks as an ordinary un-dispositioned finding** — so an un-stated refusal costs the reviewer a round exactly like silence on the existing three questions does today (`:1994-1997`, *"silence costs a round rather than saving one"*).

  This directly generalizes #3341 and #3362, reflexively: #3341 requires the **creator's** mechanism claim to cite a line; this requires the **reviewer's own refusal** to name what it tried before it counts — the same "state your evidence, don't assert" rule, applied to both sides of the exchange so the refusal itself cannot become the unfalsifiable assertion.

  **Explicit call on enforceability — accepted as a named friction-not-enforcement gap, not strengthened into a shape-check.** All three legs are self-declared: `impactIfUnfixed` is only an enum-membership check, "cost-asymmetric" is unchecked judgment, and `verificationAttempt` (as specified) is only a non-empty-string check — so a reviewer determined to refuse everything can clear all three with boilerplate ("checked, insufficient" clears leg 3; declaring `broken` clears leg 1). This is a real gap, surfaced by the skeptic pass, not a hidden one, and it is resolved the way this file already resolves every other self-declared judgment field it has (`impactIfUnfixed`, `introduced`, `worseThanBase`, `parallelizable`): fail-closed on **absence**, never machine-verified for **honesty**. Two stronger alternatives were considered and rejected: **(i)** a shape-check on `verificationAttempt` mirroring `isReproCommand` (require it look command- or citation-shaped) — rejected because it raises the bar for a *lazy* gamer only; a *determined* one fabricates a plausible-looking command or `file:line` exactly as easily as a plausible sentence, so the marginal protection does not survive the same "determined gamer" test Fork 2(c) already applies below to reject a parallel evidence system, while the cost (a new detector to build, test, and maintain) is real. **(ii)** machine-verifying the attempt's *content* — impossible for a pure function by construction, the same honest limit `isReproCommand`'s own docblock states for its own class (`:748-756`: *"it does NOT... verify it reproduces anything"*). **The actual backstop is architectural, not textual**: an `unverifiable` finding earns a round exactly like a blocker (the Ratify below), so a gamed refusal does not silently ship — it costs a round precisely as a lazy "changes requested" comment would today, and a reviewer that keeps issuing refusals a creator can trivially clear is itself a recurring pattern the **same** harvest infrastructure (*Supported by default*, below) can surface in the mirror direction — `kind: 'missing-convention'`, `area: "creator-proof / reviewer over-refusal"` is exactly as minable as the creator-side case. No new enforcement code is proposed here; the gap is named, not papered over.
- **(c) Category allowlist** — enumerate the claim *shapes* that are creator-owed (mechanism, completeness, behavioral/correctness, prevention-related) and gate on shape rather than a general test. *Rejected*: every enumerated category is itself a maintenance surface (a new claim shape appears, the list needs an edit — the same open-set problem [`#vocabulary-completeness-early`](docs/agent/platform-decisions.md#vocabulary-completeness-early) exists to avoid for standard vocabularies, applied here to a review taxonomy). #3341/#3362 remain valid as **narrower, already-shipped special cases** of the general test — not replaced, not duplicated.

**Skeptic:** SURVIVES-WITH-AMENDMENT — full attack + resolution in *Skeptic pass* below; the amendment above (the explicit enforceability call) is what the attack produced.
**Screen:** clear — full reasoning in *Two-confusion screen* below.

## Fork 2 — Should a caller-observed red-before/green-after test result outrank a citation in the creator-proof evidence ladder?

**Why this is a fork:** whether a creator's strongest possible evidence — a demonstrated test that failed on base and passes on head — should be graded as materially stronger than #3312's existing top rung (`quoted-citation`) is a genuine policy call: capping it at the same rung under-weights exactly the evidence a creator, unlike a budget-capped juror, can actually supply, but adding a rung is not free (a fifth value everywhere `EVIDENCE_STRENGTH`/`EVIDENCE_GLOSS` is asserted total over the enum, `:660-670`/`:683-691`).

*(An earlier draft of this fork also asked "should that fact live in the same enum or a separate boolean field" as a third option. The two-confusion screen flagged this as an implementation detail dressed as a policy choice — this file's own established convention already resolves it, without needing the human's judgment. Narrowed to the one real question below; the implementation-shape call is stated as a resolved default afterward, not put to a vote.)*

- **(a) Reuse `EVIDENCE_KINDS` unmodified** (`assertion` / `resolved-citation` / `repro` / `quoted-citation`). *Rejected as-is*: the ladder's own docblock states its top rung stops short of a red-before/green-after run **because of a reviewer-side budget constraint** (`:649-651`, the 693s-vs-20-minute-kill line) — a constraint that does not apply to a creator, who is expected to run the suite as ordinary development practice, not inside a review's clock. Capping a creator's strongest possible evidence at the same rung as an unrun `reproCommand` erases exactly the distinction the operator's complaint is about ("please prove me that" vs. "I asserted it").
- **(b) Extend `EVIDENCE_KINDS` with one new top rung** — call it `EVIDENCE_KINDS.VERIFIED_TEST`, ranked above `quoted-citation` in `EVIDENCE_STRENGTH`. Fed as **caller-supplied ground truth**, exactly the pattern `scope`/`sources` already establish (`classifyFindingEvidence` never trusts a juror's own word — it trusts what the *caller* hands it): the natural ground-truth source is a **mutation-check result** — `we:scripts/operations/mutation-check.mjs`'s `killed` outcome, which [#3354](docs/agent/platform-decisions.md#agent-convergence-independent-validation) already canonizes as *"a fixture counts iff there is a named line of guard code whose removal makes it fail."* This is not new evidence-collection machinery; it is one more caller-suppliable ground-truth channel, feeding a classifier that already has the shape (`classifyFindingEvidence`'s TOP-RUNG-FIRST branch, `:788-799`) to receive one. **RECOMMENDED.**

**Resolved implementation default (not part of the fork — the human is not asked to weigh this): extend the shared enum, never a parallel field.** A separate, orthogonal boolean field (`creatorVerifiedByTest: boolean`) alongside `evidenceKind` was considered and set aside as a build-time default, not offered as `(c)`: it would need its own floor/exemption logic in parallel with `admitFindingsByEvidence`'s (`:839-870`) — a second bar-comparison system next to `EVIDENCE_STRENGTH`'s, which is exactly the hand-copied-twin drift risk this same module's own docblocks warn against repeatedly (`VERDICT_STRICTNESS`'s comment on the `jury-ledger` twin that "silently went stale," `:142-147`; the totality-enforcement pattern at `:660-670`/`:683-691` that exists specifically so a rank table cannot drift from the enum it ranks). One ladder, one floor mechanism, one totality assertion — reusing what's there is cheaper *and* more correct, not merely cheaper. Once Fork 2's policy question is ratified `(b)`, this is simply how it gets built.

**A rung usable in both directions.** Extending the shared enum is not scoped only to creator-proof context — a future reviewer-side consumer that *can* supply a mutation-check result (e.g. an out-of-band probe-runner, [#2877](/backlog/2877-probe-runner-and-commit-the-probe-as-test-adversarial-reprod/)) benefits from the same rung without a second vocabulary. This is the concrete instance of the standing constraint for this session: don't discard a design option because it's expensive for *this* item alone if another consumer could reasonably want it — here the shared-enum option is not even more expensive, it is cheaper, and still generalizes.

**Skeptic:** SURVIVES — full attack in *Skeptic pass* below.
**Screen:** flagged(impl) → narrowed — full reasoning in *Two-confusion screen* below; the fix is the narrowing + resolved-default split above.

## Ratify — the disposition lives at finding granularity, gated by a precondition; no new panel-level verdict

**Forced, not a fork: the alternative loses per-finding information the existing machinery is built around.** "Unverifiable as submitted" must be encoded as a **fourth `DISPOSITIONS` member** (alongside `blocker`/`carve-out`/`nit`), produced by a **new precondition question asked before** the existing three-question routing — not as a rival to `deriveFindingDisposition`'s three-question test, but as a gate in front of it. The three existing questions (`introduced`/`worseThanBase`/`parallelizable`) presuppose the reviewer **already knows** whether the claim is a real defect and is only asking whose problem it is; an unverifiable claim fails at an earlier step — the reviewer cannot determine truth at all, so asking it to answer "did this change introduce it" is asking it to guess, which is the exact failure the operator's brief names (*"a reviewer cannot answer 'is this a blocker' about a claim it cannot verify at all"*).

Concretely: `deriveFindingDisposition({ verifiableAsSubmitted, verificationAttempt, introduced, worseThanBase, parallelizable })` checks `verifiableAsSubmitted` **first**. `false` (with a non-empty `verificationAttempt`, per Fork 1(b)) routes straight to `DISPOSITIONS.UNVERIFIABLE`, and the existing three questions are never asked — they would be answering something the reviewer just said it cannot answer. `true` or omitted falls through to today's routing, byte-stable, exactly the "strict relaxation, opt-in dial" pattern every other field in this module already follows (`disposition`, `evidenceKind`, `citationScope` — none can change a pre-existing caller's verdict without that caller opting in).

**The excluded branch — a new `VERDICTS` member (e.g. mirroring `PREVENTION_OUTSTANDING`'s escalation shape) — is not chosen, and is not even ruled out; it is deferred as a non-forced, additive follow-up.** `deriveVerdict` (`:895-910`) already reduces to `VERDICTS.CHANGES` whenever **any** outstanding finding `earnsRound` (`:902-903`), regardless of *which* disposition drove it — so the disposition-level change composes with zero edits to `deriveVerdict`, `derivePanelVerdict`, `VERDICT_STRICTNESS`, or `deriveNegotiationOutcome`. A distinct panel-level verdict would duplicate information the finding-level `disposition` already carries in full (which claim, which file, why) — the same single-sourcing discipline this file enforces everywhere else (the `frozenLookup`/totality-assertion pattern that exists specifically so two representations of one fact cannot drift apart). The render layer can — and by default should — surface the distinction in prose ("changes requested — 1 finding unverifiable as submitted, 0 confirmed defects") by reading the findings list, which is exactly what the operator's *"distinct from accept/changes"* wants a human reader to *see*, without adding a second machine-facing representation of the same fact. Minting a dedicated `VERDICTS` member later, if the render-layer distinction proves insufficient in practice, is left open as an unblocked, non-statute-affecting follow-up — never gated behind this decision.

**Earns a round — like a blocker, counted separately (answers the operator's design question 5).** An outstanding `unverifiable` finding **does** earn a round: `DISPOSITION_EARNS_ROUND[UNVERIFIABLE] = true`. Not earning a round would let a load-bearing, unproven claim land silently — exactly the escaped-defect risk this file's own `EVIDENCE_FLOOR` docblock argues against (*"an escaped defect costs more than a wasted round,"* `:704-722`), and Fork 1(b) already guarantees the claim is load-bearing before `unverifiable` even applies. But it is **never folded into the `blocker` count** — a reviewer-found bug and a creator-skipped-proof gap have different root causes and different fixes, and conflating them would corrupt exactly the per-category signal [#3318](/backlog/3318/) front A is building (adjudicated precision, seeded-defect recall, and the effective-false-positive budget are all **per finding category**; a "review-efficacy" number that can't distinguish "reviewers keep missing real bugs in area X" from "creators keep skipping proof in area X" is measuring the wrong thing).

**Skeptic:** SURVIVES — called the most solid of the three by the skeptic pass, which verified the `deriveVerdict`/`deriveNegotiationOutcome` composability claim directly against the real code rather than trusting this item's own citations. Full attack in *Skeptic pass* below.
**Screen:** clear — full reasoning in *Two-confusion screen* below.

## Skeptic pass

Run as a throwaway sub-agent (`general-purpose`, prompted only to refute — default posture "this default is wrong until it survives attack"), against the item as drafted plus the real tree in this lane, not a summary of it. Four attack axes per section — classification, merit, statute-overlap, citation-scope — plus one standing stress-test against the whole item.

**Classification axis (all three sections) — no re-routing survived.** Tried to reclassify each: could Fork 1 be a config dimension (two legitimate end-states of one knob, not a fork)? No — option (a) is affirmatively broken (no floor, degenerates to "refuses everything"), which is a forced-invariant-style exclusion, not two coexisting configs. Could Fork 2 be settled by existing precedent rather than argued fresh? No — #3312 never addressed the creator-proof case; there is no ruling to inherit. Could the Ratify actually be a fork in disguise — is minting a new `VERDICTS` member just as defensible as the disposition-only design? Attacked hardest here, and held: re-read `deriveVerdict` (`:895-910`) and `deriveNegotiationOutcome` (`:1030-1037`) directly. Both switch purely on the four-member `VERDICTS` string and never inspect `disposition` at all, so an outstanding `unverifiable` finding drives `changes` → `deriveNegotiationOutcome` continues/escalates exactly like any other `changes`, with **zero** code changes needed to either function. The Ratify's composability claim is not merely plausible — it is exactly what the code already does.

**Merit axis.**
- *Fork 1* — SURVIVES-WITH-AMENDMENT. The conjunctive test does not, by itself, stop a reviewer from gaming all three legs with boilerplate — `impactIfUnfixed` is an enum-membership check, "cost-asymmetric" is unchecked judgment, `verificationAttempt` (as first drafted) was only a non-empty-string check. The core shape still beats cost-asymmetry alone, which has no floor at all; the amendment narrows the finding to "the third leg needs an honest, not a false, enforceability claim" — folded into Fork 1 above as the explicit call, not "the test is structurally wrong."
- *Fork 2* — SURVIVES. The evidence-grading policy call (should a red-green result outrank a citation) holds on its own merits once separated from the implementation-shape question — which this pass did not flag; the two-confusion screen caught that one (below).
- *Ratify* — SURVIVES, the strongest of the three: verified directly against the real `deriveVerdict`/`deriveNegotiationOutcome` code rather than trusting the item's own citations, with no discrepancy found between the claim and the code.

**Statute-overlap axis.** Re-grepped `we:docs/agent/platform-decisions.md` independently rather than trusting the item's own list; found nothing beyond the four anchors the item already cites (`#agent-convergence-independent-validation`, `#claim-accuracy-advisory-blocks-on-impact`, `#memory-admission-verified-grounding`, `#detection-claim-matches-evidence-tier`). No new collision found.

**Citation-scope axis.** Checked the item's own #3314 move (cited as *supporting context*, not *authority*, then adopted on the merits anyway) — confirmed as the correct application of the citation-scope discipline, not a rationalization for leaning on a citation whose scope doesn't reach the case.

**The "just ceremony" stress-test — attacked, does not land.** The strongest available attack: does this add anything beyond a reviewer writing "please add a test for this" as an ordinary `changes` finding? It does, on two counts a free-text comment cannot deliver. **(1)** The disposition is **routed by code** (a new precondition check inside `deriveFindingDisposition`), not left to a reviewer's free-text framing — which is what makes it **countable**: a plain "please test this" comment is invisible to `earnsRound`'s per-category accounting and to #3318's per-category review-efficacy metrics; a `disposition: 'unverifiable'` finding is not. **(2)** It composes with the evidenceKind ladder (Fork 2) to give the creator's eventual response a **machine-checkable** proof bar, rather than leaving "was that test actually adequate" to the next reviewer's unaided re-judgment. Both are real, load-bearing differences from an ordinary comment; the attack does not land.

## Two-confusion screen

Run as a separate, fresh-context sub-agent that had not seen this session's authoring — a cold read of the item on disk, answering its fixed two questions ((1) implementation-detail-vs-policy, (2) merit-vs-prioritization) on every fork/ratify plus the *Supported by default* section.

**Fork 1 — clear.** (1) Genuine process/policy call (how permissive a reviewer's refusal may be), not an implementation detail — a consumer of the review outcome genuinely experiences a different result depending on where the line sits. (2) The three-way tradeoff (permissive / precise / maintenance-burden) is argued on correctness ("does it actually stop refuse-everything") and maintenance-surface grounds (an enumerated category list needs constant curation) — never on build cost or timing.

**Fork 2 — flagged(impl).** The original framing conflated two different questions: "should verified-test evidence be trusted as materially stronger than a citation" (a real policy call the human should weigh) and "should that fact live in the same enum or a separate boolean field" (an implementation detail this file's own existing convention — extend the shared enum rather than parallel-track, per the `VERDICT_STRICTNESS`/`jury-ledger`-twin drift-risk precedent already cited elsewhere in this item — already resolves without needing the human's judgment). **Fix applied:** the fork is narrowed to the first question only; the enum-vs-field choice is restated as a resolved implementation default in its own paragraph, not a `(c)` option inside the fork (see Fork 2 above).

**Ratify — clear.** (1) Closer to a mechanism-shape call than the two forks above, but not purely an implementation detail: *where* the answer lives (a routed, countable disposition vs. free-text) changes what the review-efficacy metrics (#3318) can measure — a genuine cross-cutting consequence, not something invisible to a consumer of the review outcome. (2) The "no new `VERDICTS` member" argument is a single-sourcing/duplication-of-fact argument, verified directly against `deriveVerdict`, not a cost argument; the item is honest that the alternative is deferred as a non-forced follow-up rather than rejected outright.

**Supported by default (harvest routing) — clear.** (1) Routing through the existing review-corpus mining pipeline and learnings pool rather than a new mechanism is exactly the kind of "which existing internal pipeline" call that belongs at the implementation layer; correctly not put in front of the human as a fork. (2) Checked closely, since "reuse what's shipped" reads suspiciously like a cost argument — held on inspection: the two-leg split (review-corpus for gate-shaped patterns, learnings-pool for convention-shaped patterns) is argued on what each pipeline is *built to do* (mine-and-score a checkable text shape vs. adjudicate cross-session recurrence), not on which is cheaper to wire up — a genuine fit-for-purpose argument, not cost dressed as merit.

## Supported by default (not decisions)

- **The creator-proof-gap record needs no new pool schema field, and — per the coordinator's correction mid-session — no new harvest mechanism at all.** Two things were considered and both dissolve to "reuse what already ships":
  - **The per-finding signal is already captured for free.** The moment `disposition: 'unverifiable'` exists as an enum member, every finding carrying it is logged exactly as every other disposition is today — to the jury ledger (`we:scripts/lib/jury-ledger.mjs`, the `FINDING` event carries the whole `finding` object, `:121`) and, once rendered, into the posted PR comment. **No new capture code is needed for this layer at all.** The mining-into-gates leg this feeds is `we:scripts/review-corpus/` — the exact mechanism [#3341](/backlog/3341/) and [#3362](/backlog/3362/) themselves went through: `we:scripts/review-corpus/mine-review-corpus.mjs` reads real PR review comments (`gh pr list`/`gh api`, read-only, `we:scripts/review-corpus/mine-review-corpus.mjs:21-37`), `we:scripts/review-corpus/gates.mjs` registers candidate detectors (`uncitedMechanismClaim` at `:647`/`:724` is #3341's own gate), and `we:scripts/review-corpus/replay-gates.mjs` scores a candidate against the recorded corpus before it graduates. A recurring `unverifiable`-disposition **shape** (e.g. "creators in area X keep asserting O(1) with no citation") is a **candidate gate**, mined and scored exactly as #3341/#3362 were — reaching `check:standards` via a route this program has already proven twice, not a new one.
  - **The convention-level, cross-session signal routes through the existing learnings pool** ([#2978](docs/agent/platform-decisions.md#memory-admission-verified-grounding), ratified) — because "is this a fresh angle or a recurring convention gap" is precisely the recurrence question one review session cannot answer alone, which is the harvest skill's own stated reason for existing (`we:skills-src/harvest-learnings/SKILL.md:15-17`). Concretely: when a reviewing session dispositions a finding `unverifiable`, it also drops **one** `we:scripts/conveyor/learnings-drop.mjs` entry — `kind: 'missing-convention'` (an existing enum member, `we:scripts/conveyor/learnings-drop.mjs:57`; no new `kind` is minted, see next bullet), `area` naming the creator-facing artifact family the gap implies (e.g. `"creator-proof / dispatch-brief"`, `"creator-proof / prep-checklist"`), `summary`/`suggestion` generalized exactly as the schema already forces for every other kind (no path, no code, no diff — the pool's existing privacy-by-schema design, `we:scripts/conveyor/learnings-drop.mjs:12-16`). `npm run harvest`'s existing dedup-by-`area`, session-count ranking, and cause-synthesis (ratified as a design requirement by #2978 clause 2, *"a cluster's output is a design-level story naming that cause"* — the mechanism itself, [#3019](/backlog/3019-harvest-synthesizes-a-cluster-into-a-cause-story-not-an-elec/), is filed but not yet built) route a **recurring** convention gap to a `we:backlog/` story that edits the named dispatch brief, prep checklist, or skill doc — the existing "fix with an owner → backlog" rule (`we:skills-src/harvest-learnings/SKILL.md:113-114`), unchanged.
  - **These two legs are not rivals — they serve different destinations for different reasons.** A GATE-shaped recurring pattern (a checkable text shape, like #3341's citation requirement) belongs in the review-corpus pipeline, which is built to mine and score exactly that. A CONVENTION-shaped recurring pattern (a working-style gap no deterministic check can see, like "the dispatch brief for area X never asks for a repro") belongs in the learnings pool, which is built to adjudicate recurrence across sessions. Routing a specific record to one or the other is a per-incident judgment call (does this generalize to a checkable text shape, or only to a process instruction?), the same judgment `we:skills-src/harvest-learnings/SKILL.md`'s own routing step already asks of every candidate — not a new decision this item needs to make.
- **No new `kind` value in the learnings-pool schema.** `KINDS = ['friction', 'missing-convention', 'doc-gap', 'skill-gap', 'improvement']` (`we:scripts/conveyor/learnings-drop.mjs:57`) already has a member — `missing-convention` — that fits a creator-proof gap exactly ("a convention the creator should have followed and didn't"); occasionally `improvement` fits better when the gap names something a **dispatch brief** should proactively state rather than a convention a creator skipped. Minting a dedicated `kind: 'unverifiable-claim'` was considered and set aside: harvest already clusters near-duplicates by content (`area`/`summary`), not by `kind` alone, so a dedicated kind would buy sharper *counting* at the cost of a schema change every future consumer that switches on `kind` must handle — real ongoing cost with no signal the existing five-member enum can't already carry via `area`. Revisit only if real harvest volume shows the `missing-convention` bucket needs splitting — a measured call, not a speculative one now.
- **"Attributable" means attributable to a *role* (creator-side vs. reviewer-side), not to a specific PR, session, or person — and the pool's schema is deliberately incapable of the latter.** The allow-list (`we:scripts/conveyor/learnings-drop.mjs:60`, `ALLOWED_KEYS = ['kind', 'summary', 'area', 'suggestion']`) carries no `file`/`path`/`pr`/`session-content` field by design — *"if the schema has no field for it, it can't leak"* (`:12-16`), the same minimality this repo already treats as a privacy requirement ahead of the eventual multi-tenant product-feedback channel, [#2610]. A creator-proof-gap record does not need a new field to satisfy the operator's "attributable" ask: the role distinction (this is a creator-side gap, not a reviewer-side one) is fully expressible in free text inside `area`/`kind`, exactly how every other axis in this four-key schema is already represented (there is no dedicated `severity` field either — severity lives in the prose). A new field was considered and rejected on this same minimality principle.

## Statute-overlap check

`we:docs/agent/platform-decisions.md` was grepped for same-subject anchors beyond [`#agent-convergence-independent-validation`](docs/agent/platform-decisions.md#agent-convergence-independent-validation) (already cited above as the direct sibling #3354 amended).

- **Adopted on the merits anyway.** Fork 1(b)'s "load-bearing" leg was drafted, independently, as a bespoke concept before this grep — and #3314's closing instruction is exactly the fix: reuse the **existing typed field** (`impactIfUnfixed`) rather than inventing a parallel "load-bearing" concept. Fork 1's recommended default was rewritten to cite `impactIfUnfixed >= UNVERIFIABLE_IMPACT_BAR` directly, honoring #3314's instruction on the merits even though its literal authority does not reach this case. **No collision, and better-grounded for the reading.**

**[`#memory-admission-verified-grounding`](docs/agent/platform-decisions.md#memory-admission-verified-grounding) (#2978, ratified) governs the harvest routing directly** and is cited, not merely the skill doc that implements it — see *Supported by default* above. **[`#detection-claim-matches-evidence-tier`](docs/agent/platform-decisions.md#detection-claim-matches-evidence-tier) (#1673) — no collision, related idiom only.** It governs a *tool's printed claim* about live-page conformance detection ("the strength of the claim must match the evidence tier it actually has") — a different subject (a tool's own output-phrasing, not a creator's code claim under review) but the same recurring idiom in this repo: don't let an assertion outrun its evidence.
